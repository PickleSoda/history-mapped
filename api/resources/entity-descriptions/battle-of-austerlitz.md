The Battle of Austerlitz was fought on 2 December 1805, a few kilometres south-east of Brno in what is now Czechia, between Napoleon's French army and the combined forces of Russia and Austria. It is often called the Battle of the Three Emperors, because Napoleon and Tsar Alexander I were present, and Napoleon wrongly believed that Francis of Austria was too. The French fielded roughly 65,000 to 75,000 men and the Allies roughly 73,000 to 89,000. It ended in one of the most complete victories of the Napoleonic wars.

The battle was part of the War of the Third Coalition. After the French surrounded an Austrian army at Ulm and entered Vienna, Napoleon found himself far from home with stretched supply lines, and the Allies gathered in Moravia. Russian and Austrian leaders pressed for a fight, against the caution of Francis and of Kutuzov, the nominal commander. Weyrother's plan called for a main drive against the apparently weak French right to cut the road to Vienna, with the Russian Guard in reserve and Bagration guarding the Allied right.

Napoleon had studied the ground, and he invited exactly that attack. He abandoned the Pratzen Heights, the gentle plateau at the centre of the field, and thinned his right along the Goldbach stream, where the villages of Telnitz and Sokolnitz stood. Most of his troops waited in the dead ground near Puntowitz and Girzikowitz. Davout's corps marched about 110 km from Vienna in 48 hours to reinforce the right. Lannes and Murat held the north near the Olmütz road, and Napoleon kept the Guard and Bernadotte's corps in reserve.

The fighting began at about 8 a.m. at Telnitz, where the Allied left columns threw the French out, were thrown out in turn by Davout's first arrivals, and spent the morning in a tangle around Telnitz and Sokolnitz. Meanwhile the Tsar ordered Kutuzov's fourth column off the Pratzen. At around 9 a.m., once the fog thinned, Napoleon released Soult's IV Corps against the heights. St Hilaire's division was driven back once and then cleared the summit with the bayonet, while Vandamme took the Old Vineyards. The Russian Imperial Guard counterattacked and took a French standard, but the French Guard cavalry and the flank deployment of Drouet's division broke it. In the north, Murat's cuirassiers beat Liechtenstein's cavalry and Lannes drove Bagration off. Napoleon then turned south and trapped the Allied left. Part of the retreating force went over the frozen Satschan ponds, and the scale of the drownings is disputed.

The Allies lost about 36,000 men and 186 guns, and the French about 9,000. Austria signed an armistice on 4 December and the Treaty of Pressburg on 26 December, ceding territory and paying 40 million francs. The Russian army withdrew intact. The Holy Roman Empire ended in 1806, Napoleon built the Confederation of the Rhine, and Prussia, offended, declared war on France that year. Historians still debate how far Napoleon planned the whole battle in advance and how many men were really lost in the ponds.
